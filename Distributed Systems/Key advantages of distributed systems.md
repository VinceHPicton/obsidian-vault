
1. Horizontal scalability
This is the huge one, you're able to add new separate machines running your system, meaning you can scale beyond what 1 machine could ever handle.

2. Availability and fault tolerance
By having multiple machines, you're able to keep your service running even if 1 or many nodes fail

3. Geographic proximity to users
Because you can have many machines, you can place them near your users globally, reducing latency.

4. Workload specialisation
Since you have multiple systems communicating via network calls, there's no reason they all have to use the same language or database, so you can choose the best tool for each job

5. Organisational scaling
Different teams can own and deploy separate services independently, and can specialise their domain knowledge to their part of the system, without needing to know the depths of how other parts work, meaning deeper expertise in the pieces they work on.
